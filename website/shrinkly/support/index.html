Common questions

How do I get a video under a size limit?
  1. Choose the video, or several.
  2. Open the size options and tap a preset such as WhatsApp or Email, or type the size in MB.
  3. Check the estimate shown under the slider. If it says the target is too low for the length of the clip, raise the target, lower the resolution, or trim the clip.
  4. Start. When it finishes, look at the result sizes and use Compare to check the quality, then save or share.

The size you ask for is a target. The real file is usually close to it, and can be a little over or under. If a file must stay below a hard limit, set the target a little below the limit.

Why can't my video be made smaller?

A video has a lowest useful size. The file size is roughly the bitrate times the length, so a long video squeezed into a few megabytes ends up with a bitrate so low that the picture turns into blocks. Shrinkly keeps a quality floor instead of giving you an unwatchable file, and tells you when your target is below it.

To go lower, try one or more of these: a lower resolution (720p or 480p), a lower frame rate (30 or 24 instead of 60), HEVC instead of H.264, trimming the clip, muting the audio, or splitting a long video in two. Clips that were already compressed hard, such as videos downloaded from social media, have little left to give.

My result is a bit bigger than my target

Size targets are aimed at, not guaranteed to the byte. Complex scenes, a high frame rate and audio can all push the result over. Lower the target a few percent and run it again, or choose a lower resolution. Your original is never changed, so you can try again as often as you like.

Which video formats work?

Shrinkly opens MP4, MOV, M4V, 3GP, HEVC and ProRes video that iOS plays by itself, and many formats iOS does not play: MKV, WebM, AVI, WMV, ASF, FLV, MPEG, VOB, TS, M2TS, MTS, OGV, DV and others, through a built-in open-source engine. It saves the result as MP4 or MOV with H.264 or HEVC video and AAC audio.

If a file does not open, write to us with the file extension and, if you can, the program that made it. We cannot promise that every unusual file will open, but we want to know about the ones that do not.

Why does it say my video is protected?

Videos bought, rented or downloaded from streaming and download stores are protected by digital rights management (DRM). Only the store's own app can play them, and no other app can read them. Shrinkly cannot process these files, and it does not try to bypass the protection. Videos you recorded yourself, or files without protection, work normally.

Why am I asked to watch an ad?

Shrinkly is free, and ads pay for it. Before a save, share or export, the app offers one short video that you choose to watch. A batch of videos needs one ad. If you choose Not now, nothing is saved and you can try again any time. If no ad is available, for example when you are offline or your ad privacy choice does not allow ads, the export goes ahead without one. Compressing and previewing never need an ad.

Compressing is slow, or it stops

Long videos, 4K video and HEVC take more time, more memory and more battery, and a warm phone slows down to cool off. Keep Shrinkly open and the screen on until the job is finished, make sure your device has free storage of at least the size of the original, and try a lower resolution if a very large file keeps failing. You can cancel at any time and your original stays as it was.

Where did my compressed video go?

If you chose Save to Photos, it is in your photo library, at the end of the Recents album. If you chose Files, it is in the folder you picked. The original is untouched. Shrinkly can offer to delete the original after saving, and only does so if you confirm. Check the new file first.

H.264 or HEVC?

H.264 plays almost everywhere, including older phones, computers, TVs and every messaging app. HEVC (also called H.265) gives a smaller file at the same quality, but some older devices, websites and apps cannot play it. If you are not sure, choose H.264. If you need the smallest possible file for an Apple device, choose HEVC.

Does it remove my location from the video?

Yes, if you turn on the option to strip location and other metadata in the advanced options. The compressed copy is then written without them. Your original keeps its metadata.

I want to change my ad or tracking choice

Open Settings in the app and look for the Ad privacy choices row, which appears where the law requires it. You can also change tracking in iOS Settings under Privacy and Security, then Tracking, and reset your advertising identifier there.

I saw an ad that seems wrong

Write to us at the address above. Tell us roughly when and where in the app you saw it and what it showed. A screenshot helps. We block ad categories and advertisers through Google AdMob where we can.

I have an idea for a preset or a feature

Send it. We read every message, though we cannot promise to build every idea.
